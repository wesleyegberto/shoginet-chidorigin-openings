Extending further from High Mino (Taka-Mino), advancing with ▽８三銀 followed by ▽７二金—where the Mino shape shifts diagonally to the right, placing a Silver directly above the King—creates the ultimate evolved form of the Mino Castle: the "Silver Crown" (Ginkan-gakoi). In Figure 3, Sente has likewise built a Silver Crown, resulting in an even (gokaku) position.

However, in a symmetrical position like this where both Kings are solidly protected, it becomes difficult for either side to make a move, which can sometimes lead to a situation called Sennichite (Repetition Draw). Sennichite occurs when the exact same board position—including piece placement and hands—appears four times. In that case, the game is declared a draw with no winner, and in official play, the game is replayed with the players swapping Sente and Gote. However, if the repetition occurs entirely through a series of continuous checks, the checking player loses unless they change their moves.

Besides Sennichite, another way a game can end in a draw with no winner is Jishōgi (Impasse). This happens when both Kings enter the opponent's territory (Nyūgyoku, or "King Entry") and neither side can deliver checkmate. After capturing each other's pieces, the players reach a point where no progress can be made.

However, in the case of Jishōgi (Impasse), once both Kings have entered enemy territory and it becomes impossible to deliver checkmate or capture any more pieces, whichever player fails to reach the required point threshold loses.

Excluding the King, major pieces (Rooks and Bishops) are worth 5 points each, and minor pieces are worth 1 point each. Counting all pieces in hand and on the board, a player must have at least 24 points. If both players satisfy this condition, the game ends in a draw.
