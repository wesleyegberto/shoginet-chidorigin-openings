Shifting the King from ▽６一玉 to ▽５二玉 toward the center, ensuring Gote can launch counterattacks regardless of whether Sente strikes from the left or right, is the sequence where the Kazaguruma truly shows its worth.

Depending on how Sente chooses to attack, Gote's main strategy from this position is either to aim for an Entering King (Nyūgyoku, 入玉) or, upon capturing a Knight or Lance, to launch a fierce counterattack along the 9th file. However, if Sente refuses to move, initiating an attack from the Kazaguruma side becomes overambitious. Because its primary goal is essentially to force a repetition (Sennichite, 千日手), it is a strategy best suited for playing as Gote (the second player).

This New Kazaguruma is not a Central Rook setup, but rather a Static Rook variation known as Migi-Gyoku (Right King, 右玉). When Sente exchanges Pawns on the 3rd file with the Bishop, Gote opens the Bishop diagonal with ▽４五歩 and launches a counterattack along the 8th file; by Figure 3, Gote holds a superior position. The core secret of this strategy is to confuse the opponent and gain a strategic advantage (Sakusen-gachi) by leaving open the option to transition into a Ranging Rook midway through.

In the sequence, after Sente captures Gote's ▽８六歩 with ▲同歩, Gote follows up by striking again with ▽８五歩; this tactical motif is called a Tsugibu (Continuing Pawn / 継ぎ歩). If Sente answers with ▲同歩, Gote plays ▽同桂; after Sente plays ▲８八銀, Gote drops ▽８六歩 and aims for ▽６五歩 next, yielding a clear advantage for Gote.

Since the New Kazaguruma diverges from Central Rook, we will limit ourselves to this brief introduction. Mastering either Kazaguruma or New Kazaguruma requires considerable playing strength. Thus, while it is not a strategy heavily recommended for beginners, it serves as a valuable example to show that such unique approaches exist within the Central Rook spectrum.
