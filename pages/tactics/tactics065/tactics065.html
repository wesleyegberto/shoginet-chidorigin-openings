We have moved through the sequence quickly down to Figure 1. Gote pulls the Bishop back to ▽４二角 and leaps the Knight with ▽３三桂, allowing the 1st-rank Rook to rotate freely anywhere from the 2nd file all the way to the 8th file. This tactic was named Kazaguruma (Windmill) because the Rook spins smoothly back and forth across the bottom rank like a windmill. Its ultimate master principle is to thoroughly neutralize the opponent's attack: when the opponent pushes forward, Gote counters naturally by using the opponent's own momentum, but never initiates an attack first.

In Figure 2, Sente's floating Silver on ５七 is pinned down under the gaze of Gote's Rook on ５一. Furthermore, if Sente makes a reckless attack and hands over Knights, Gote's edge assault on the 9th file becomes devastating. At this point, Gote has already achieved a strategically advantageous position (Sakusen-gachi).

Since this leaves the Static Rook side far too helpless, let us try a different variation. Instead of Sente playing ▲５九金 following Gote's ▽６二金 in Figure 1, Sente can opt for ▲５八金.
