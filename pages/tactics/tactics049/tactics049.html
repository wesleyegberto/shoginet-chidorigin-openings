No-Guard Strategy (Nō-gādo Senpō, ノーガード戦法) [2]

In this chapter, we will explain the methods to crush the No-Guard Strategy—a surprise attack by Gote stemming from a Yokofudori setup.

In the Double Wing Attack (Aigakari) structure, Gote's ▽８四飛 shape becomes an attack target and is dangerous. The sequence up to Figure 2 is one such example, but even with variations from Figure 1, Sente—being able to strike first—holds the advantage. In the sequence, if Gote takes with ▽同角 instead of ▽同金 against Sente's ▲３三同桂成, Sente drops ▲３四桂; when the King escapes, Sente plays ▲３三角成, and after ▽同金, drops ▲６六角, yielding a result virtually identical to Figure 2.

Figure 4

When Gote retreats deep to ▽８二飛 in response to ▲８七歩, there is no special countermeasure required and it seems likely to become a standard Double Wing Attack (Aigakari) game. However, the moment Sente exchanges the Rook-pawns and Gote defends with ▽３二金, Sente has the option to play ▲５八玉. Surprisingly, Gote is in trouble in this Figure 4 position.

Naturally, Sente's objective is to take the side pawn with ▲３四飛 on the next move, but Gote has already lost the option of defending with ▽２三歩. Since moving Gold or Silver pieces is dangerous in a Yokofudori setup, Gote could try pushing ▽８六歩 once again; however, please note that because Sente spent a move on ▲５八玉, most of Gote's tactical aims explained so far no longer work.

From the above, we can see that responding with ▲８七歩 to ask where Gote's Rook intends to go is an effective countermeasure against the No-Guard Strategy.

This concludes our coverage of the Yokofudori section for now. While some parts may have been difficult for beginners to grasp, Yokofudori positions frequently appear in televised games. My intention was to summarize the strategy as clearly as possible, hoping that even a basic understanding of its outline would make watching those games even more enjoyable.
