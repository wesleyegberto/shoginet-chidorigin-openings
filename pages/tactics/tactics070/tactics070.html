5五 Dragon Central Rook Strategy (Ryū Nakabisha, ５五龍中飛車戦法)

There is a manga by manga artist Jiro Tsunoda titled 5五 no Ryū (5五の龍) that depicts young boys striving to become professional Shogi players. In this work, the protagonist, Ryū Komagata, uses his signature strategy known as the 5五 Dragon Central Rook (5五 Ryū Nakabisha).

The core essence of the 5-Five Dragon Central Rook is to refuse Gote's Pawn exchange on the 8th file by responding to ▽８四歩 and ▽８五歩 with ▲９六歩 followed by ▲９七角. Reaching the position shown in Figure 1 naturally raises a question: how should Sente handle Gote pushing ▽９四歩 to aim for a break with ▽９五歩?

If Gote plays cautiously to avoid an immediate clash, the game naturally progresses to Figure 3. From here, Sente holds the initiative with the constant threat to ignite the battle via ▲５四歩 at any moment. Furthermore, Gote has already run out of effective constructive moves, making this a position of clear strategic advantage (Sakusen-gachi) for Sente. Controlling and clamping down on the board centered around the ５五 square was the true goal of the 5-Five Dragon Central Rook strategy all along.

In the move sequence, at the point where Gote played ▽４三銀 against ▲６六銀, one might wonder if Gote could strike with ▽９五歩 since Sente's Rook line along the rank has been blocked. However, Sente can simply respond by advancing ▲６五銀. If Gote captures with ▽９六歩, Sente jumps out with ▲７五角; steering the game into a chaotic melee like this still keeps the tempo entirely in favor of the 5五 Dragon Central Rook (Figure 4).
