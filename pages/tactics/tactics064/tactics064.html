Horned Silver Central Rook (Tsuno-Gin Nakabisha, ツノ銀中飛車戦法) [2]

In the previous chapter, we covered the offensive and defensive possibilities of Climbing Silver against Gote's Tsuno-Gin Central Rook from the ▲５七銀左 setup. In this chapter, we will examine Tsuno-Gin's countermeasures when Sente adopts a different, highly effective strategy from that same ▲５七銀左 setup.

Sente's strategy of playing ▲４七銀 followed by ▲３八飛, deploying Double Silvers to target the head of Gote's Bishop, is known as Sodebisha (Sleeve Rook, 袖飛車) and serves as a powerful option for Static Rook against Central Rook. Pushing ▽４五歩 against ▲３八飛, as done in the previous chapter, proves overambitious here because Sente's central Double Silvers provide a robust defense. Instead, Gote's most effective countermeasure is pulling back with ▽３一金 and intercepting Sente's ▲３五歩 Pawn exchange by swinging the Rook with ▽３二飛.

Figure 3


Against Sente pulling back with ▲３八飛, Gote aggressively clears the position with ▽４五歩. If Sente responds to this by attempting a full Rook and Bishop exchange starting with ▲３三角成 instead of ▲６六歩, the structural difference leaves the Static Rook side at a disadvantage during rapid attack battles—a principle common across all Ranging Rook variations.

When Gote plays ▽４六歩, Sente responds with ▲同銀左; capturing with ▲同銀右 instead leaves Sente's Rook undefended, inviting Gote's punishing ▽６六角. Pushing forward from ▽６五歩 down to Figure 4 results in a position where Gote holds a distinct advantage. With its resilient defense and capacity for dynamic board-clearing counterattacks, this strategy possesses considerable charm. Nevertheless, Tsuno-Gin Central Rook is rarely played today among professionals or amateurs alike. This shift occurred due to the emergence of a strategy that served as its ultimate natural enemy.

That natural enemy is none other than Ibisha Anaguma (Static Rook Anaguma, 居飛車穴熊). In Figure 4, battle breaks out along the 7th file. Even if Sente sacrifices a piece along the way, their enclosure proves vastly superior in solidness compared to Gote's defense—even though both castles utilize two Generals (Gold and Silver). Because of this overwhelming structural integrity, Sente frequently pulls ahead and wins the endgame speed race.
