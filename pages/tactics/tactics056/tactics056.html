Opposing Rook - 4th-move ▽3三 Bishop Formation (4手目▽3三角型向かい飛車戦法) [1]

In this chapter, we will introduce an off-book play (rikisen, 力戦) strategy where Gote aims for a sequence similar to the Demon Killer Opposing Rook (Onikoroshi Mukaibisha, 鬼殺し向かい飛車) from the previous chapter, starting when Sente opens the Bishop's diagonal on move 1 with ▲７六歩.

Moving ▽３三角 on move 4 is a strategy with few practical examples, but it is one that has been played in professional games. After Sente exchanges Bishops, Gote responds with ▽同桂; if Sente plays ▲２五歩 here, it transposes into the basic formation of the Demon Killer Opposing Rook from the previous chapter, but Sente also comes prepared with counter-innovations.

In this Figure 1, if Gote plays ▽２二飛, this time Sente drops ▲６五角, leaving Gote in trouble. Since the ５七 square is already protected, ▽４五桂 serves no purpose, and even if Gote drops ▽４四角, Sente defends easily with ▲７七桂. That's right—▲６八玉 in Figure 1 is Sente's countermeasure to block Gote's ▽２二飛.

The countermeasure against Sente's ▲６五角 is for Gote to drop a Bishop with ▽６五角 instead. Dropping a Bishop to ６五 (or ４五 for Sente)—a square unreachable from the starting position of ２二 (or ８八 for Sente) without promoting—to capture the Pawn on ７六 (or ３四 for Sente) where the Bishop's diagonal was opened is called Suichigai Kaku (Wrong-File Bishop, 筋違い角) because it drops the Bishop onto a line different from its natural file, and it is a strategy in its own right. By adding this maneuver into the mix, Gote realizes ▽２二飛, reaching Figure 2.

Both the Demon Slayer Opposing Rook from previous chapters and this Move 4 ▽３三角 style can be said to be strategies made possible by Sente exchanging Bishops. Naturally, the question arises: what happens if Sente does not exchange Bishops?
