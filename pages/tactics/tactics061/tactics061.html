From Figure 2, Sente fortifies their King, advances with ▲５六銀, and aims for a straightforward attacking line with ▲６五歩 ▽同歩 ▲同銀 ▽６四歩 ▲７四歩. Realizing it would be disastrous to let this plan succeed, Gote tries to repel the Silver with ▽５五歩, but Sente advances further with ▲４五銀. While an advancing Silver near the opponent's King (Gyokutō-gin) can be trapped if played recklessly, in this specific position Gote has focused their defenses on the left flank, leaving their King's side weak and making Sente's Silver advance highly effective.

The sequence down to Figure 4 is one example of how the play proceeds. Gote's ▽５六歩 in response to ▲６五歩 is an attempt to clear the Bishop, which had become a burden. After exchanging Bishops and playing ▲５六飛, Gote would love to defend first with ▽３二金, but Sente immediately counters with ▲７一角; against ▽６二飛, Sente plays ▲５三飛成, and it is all over. In Figure 4, Sente threatens checkmate on the next turn with ▲２三銀成, and even knowing it is coming, Gote has no defensive moves available. A position where checkmate cannot be prevented even with a free turn is called Hisshi (Brinkmate, 必至).
