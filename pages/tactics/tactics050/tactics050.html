Opposing Rook Strategy (Mukaibisha, 向かい飛車戦法) [1]

Among Ranging Rook (Furibisha) openings, we introduce the Opposing Rook (Mukaibisha) strategy, which makes it difficult for Static Rook (Ibisha) to initiate a fast attack (kyūsen) and possesses strong counterattacking power.

Basically, you cannot transition into Opposing Rook (Mukaibisha) unless the opponent advances their Rook-pawn to the 5th rank (the 5th rank counting from their side, i.e., ▲２五歩). Nowadays, in the early game, players often leave the Rook-pawn advanced by only one square to restrict the opponent's choices; however, if you know the opponent is a dedicated Static Rook player who rarely plays Ranging Rook, pushing to ▲２五歩 and forcing them into ▽３三角 can be a viable strategy. In Figure 1, Sente's move ▲６八玉 is a careless blunder.

Specific to Opposing Rook, moving the King up with ▲６八玉 in this setup is a blunder. Here, when Gote pushes ▽２四歩, taking with ▲同歩 followed by Gote's ▽同角 places the King in check; through Figure 2, Gote captures the Rook cleanly, securing a winning advantage (shōsei).

Even if Sente takes with ▲同飛 when Gote plays ▽同角, and after Gote responds with ▽同飛 Sente tries dropping ▲１五角 for a quasi-check-and-Rook fork, ▽２五飛 is an excellent defense. Following ▲２四角 and ▽同飛, the 2nd file cannot be defended, leaving Gote in a winning position all the same (Figure 3). Furthermore, if Sente avoids taking ▽２四歩 and allows Gote to take with ▽２五歩, Gote counterattacks along the Rook's file with no way for Sente to defend, leading to essentially the same outcome.

Figure 3


Against Opposing Rook, failing to defend by either moving the King up without pushing the 5th-file Pawn or pushing ▲４六歩 to neutralize the checking line will lead to disaster. The variations shown through Figure 2 and Figure 3 are considered fundamental common knowledge in the early game of Opposing Rook tactics.
