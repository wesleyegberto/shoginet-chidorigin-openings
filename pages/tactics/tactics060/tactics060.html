"Meriken" (American) Opposing Rook (Meriken Mukaibisha, メリケン向かい飛車戦法) [1]

We introduce the Meriken Opposing Rook (Meriken Mukaibisha, メリケン向かい飛車), created by Kimimochi Yokoyama, a well-known top amateur player nationwide.

When Sente shows signs of playing Ranging Rook (振り飛車) and Gote, wishing to avoid Opposing Rook, holds back their Rook-file Pawn at ▽８四歩, Sente plays ▲７五歩 aiming for the Ishida Style (石田流) with ▲７八飛 followed by ▲７六飛 next. If Gote does not play ▽８五歩 here, Sente achieves that formation, so Gote pushes the Pawn—allowing Sente to switch to Opposing Rook with ▲７七角 and ▲８八飛.

Sente forms the Aggressive Mukaibisha structure with ▲７八金, and it seems this line was named "Meriken Mukaibisha" because the thrust Pawn on ７五 resembles wearing brass knuckles (known as Meriken Sakkus in Japanese) on one's fist. Launching a counterattack along the Rook's file with ▲８六歩 while leaving the King in its starting position is identical to the Aggressive Mukaibisha covered in Chapter 51. However, after ▽同歩, Sente forcefully challenges Gote to a Rook exchange with ▲同飛 in Figure 2 - and surprisingly, Sente already holds the advantage here.

Exchanging Rooks leaves Sente's camp with no open squares for Gote to drop a Rook, placing Gote at a disadvantage; Gote therefore declines the exchange with ▽８五歩. However, after pulling back with ▲８八飛, Sente follows up with ▲７六銀, aiming directly at Gote's Pawn on ８五. This is precisely the benefit of pushing ▲７五歩 earlier.

Gote resorts to desperate defensive measures: playing ▽９四歩 followed by ▽９三桂 to somehow defend the ８五 Pawn, and then pushing ▽６四歩 to target a breakthrough with ▽６五歩 if Sente's Silver on ７六 moves. Nevertheless, after Sente solidifies the 6th file with ▲６七金 and attacks the head of Gote's Knight with ▲９五歩 to reach Figure 3, Sente holds a decisive advantage.

Defending against the Meriken Mukaibisha's Rook-file counterattack with the standard ▽８五歩 proved unfavorable for Gote, as Sente immediately targeted that Pawn with ▲７六銀. Consequently, Gote must change their defensive approach.
